# Data provenance: real fields vs. synthetic fields

The simulator is built on `../../parking_data.csv` (Parkopedia, Bellevue WA, 1 km radius:
1,104 rows, 24 distinct `location_id`s, 53 collection snapshots, 2026-03-20 13:20 →
2026-03-21 13:52) and on `data/osm_parking_bellevue.json` (OpenStreetMap, ODbL).

Every per-garage attribute falls into one of the categories below. All derivation is
**deterministic** — the same `location_id` always produces the same attributes, independent of
the episode seed, because a physical garage does not change depending on which simulation run
it appears in.

## Real fields, taken from the price feed unaltered

- `location_id`
- Median / minimum / maximum price per garage (the `price` column)
- **Availability fraction** = `snapshots in which this garage appeared / 53`.

  This is the most informative hidden signal in the dataset. If a garage returns **no quote**
  in some snapshots, the most plausible explanation is that it was **full** — Parkopedia does
  not return a quote for a garage with no bookable space — rather than an API error.
  Appearance counts range from 17/53 (about 32%) to 53/53 (100%), so the column naturally
  encodes how frequently each garage is contended.
- `requires_print_pass` / `requires_display_pass` / `cancellation_notice` (retained verbatim;
  not yet used by the simulator, kept for future extension)

## Coordinates: changed from synthetic to real (2026-10-05)

Coordinates were originally generated by hashing `location_id` and sampling uniformly by area
inside a 1 km disc. Compared against OpenStreetMap, that model was **measurably wrong**: real
parking sits at a median of 554 m from the destination anchor, while uniform-by-area placement
puts it at 744 m (KS test D=0.301, p=1.2e−06). Because the utility function penalises walking
beyond a tolerance, the bias inflated walk costs in every arm.

Coordinates now come from `data/osm_parking_bellevue.json`: **156 real OSM parking features**
inside the same 1 km disc the scraper queried, frozen into the repository so the simulator
never depends on a live API and results stay reproducible if OSM changes.

**The limitation that must be stated plainly.** The 24 garages are assigned coordinates by
shuffling the 156 candidates with a fixed seed (20260320) and dealing them out in sorted
`location_id` order. So the fleet's **spatial distribution is real** — clustering, radial
distribution, distances to the destination — **but which garage sits at which coordinate is
arbitrary**. Parkopedia `location_id`s cannot be matched to OSM features (only 8 of the 156
are even named), and claiming such a correspondence would be a fabricated data join.

Consequences are reported in full as Amendment A-2 in
[PRE_REGISTRATION.md](PRE_REGISTRATION.md), including the parts that contradicted my own
prediction: all three Experiment B conclusions kept their direction, but the greedy-vs-FIFO
gap narrowed by 19–37%, because real parking clusters and clustering is what erodes Greedy's
advantage.

## Fields that are still synthetic (deterministic, not real data)

| Field | How it is derived | Why it is synthetic |
|---|---|---|
| `capacity` (number of spaces / lanes) | `round(BASE_CAPACITY * availability_fraction)` with `BASE_CAPACITY=40`, clipped to a floor of 3 | Neither source carries capacity. The availability fraction is used as a defensible real-data proxy. Only 6 of 156 OSM features have a `capacity` tag. |
| `is_ev` | `hash(location_id) % 5 == 0` (about 20% of garages flagged EV) | The price feed has no EV field, and OSM has almost none either — EV tagging is sparser than capacity tagging. The 20% share is an assumption modelled on the typical proportion of CampusPark's production `isEV` field. |

This limitation is recorded in
[research_proposal_llm_allocation.md](../research_proposal_llm_allocation.md) §7, Threats to
Validity: 24 garages over roughly 24.5 hours is a thin real-world sample, and the capacity and
EV dimensions are synthetic. Every synthetic derivation is deterministic, reproducible, and
documented here — none of it was tuned to make any allocation strategy look better.
